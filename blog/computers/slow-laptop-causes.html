Common Reasons Why a Laptop Becomes Slow

Home → Knowledge Center → Slow Laptop Causes

Nothing is more frustrating than a laptop that takes five minutes just to open a web browser. If your once-speedy machine is now crawling, you are not alone. Here are the most common culprits we see at Saikrupa Computers.

1. You're Still Using a Mechanical Hard Drive (HDD)

If your laptop is a few years old, it might still have an old spinning Hard Disk Drive (HDD). These mechanical drives degrade over time. The absolute best upgrade you can perform is replacing the HDD with a Solid State Drive (SSD). An SSD can make an old laptop boot up in seconds instead of minutes.

2. Not Enough RAM (Memory)

RAM is your laptop's short-term memory. If you like keeping 30 browser tabs open while streaming music and working in Excel, 4GB of RAM won't cut it anymore. Upgrading to 8GB or 16GB of RAM is usually a quick and inexpensive fix for heavy multitaskers.

3. Thermal Throttling (Overheating)

Laptops have tiny fans. Over months of use, these fans suck in dust and hair, clogging the cooling vents. When the processor gets too hot, it automatically slows itself down (thermal throttling) to prevent melting. A professional internal cleaning and fresh thermal paste will solve this.

4. Too Many Startup Programs

Many applications set themselves to launch automatically when you turn on the computer. Check your Task Manager (Windows) and disable unnecessary startup apps like Skype or Spotify if you don't need them immediately upon booting.
